Here's a number to get your attention: **up to 40% off cruise fares**, plus up to $500 in instant savings per cabin, 50% off deposits, and free third and fourth guests on select sailings. That's the real offer Princess Cruises put on the table this year during what the industry calls "wave season" — and if you've never timed a cruise booking around it, you've been leaving money on the table.

## What Wave Season Actually Is

Wave season is cruise-industry shorthand for the window when lines aggressively discount future sailings to lock in bookings. Traditionally it runs **January through March**, but here's the bit that matters for planning: that window has been stretching. Promotions now commonly start as early as November and run through to June, so "wave season" is less a hard deadline and more a long, favourable stretch you can shop within.

Why do the lines do it? Simple cash-flow logic. Northern Hemisphere winter is when their core market is stuck indoors dreaming of the ocean, so lines front-load bookings with their biggest discounts of the year to guarantee full ships months in advance. You don't need to be freezing in a Melbourne July to benefit — the fare drops apply to itineraries departing everywhere, including sailings around Australia and New Zealand, not just European or Caribbean routes.

## What's Actually on the Table

Real perks I've seen stacked during recent wave seasons:

- Base fare discounts up to 40%
- Instant per-cabin savings up to $500 on longer voyages (17+ nights)
- Deposits cut by 50%, so you commit less upfront
- Free or heavily discounted fares for a third or fourth guest sharing your cabin
- Bundled extras: Wi-Fi, drinks packages, gratuities, or onboard credit thrown in rather than sold separately
- Cruise-and-airfare combo deals that can beat booking each leg yourself

## How to Actually Get the Good Deal, Not the Fake One

A few rules I stick to, because cruise lines are not shy about theatre:

1. **Track the price for a few months before wave season starts.** You need a genuine baseline to know whether "40% off" is real or whether the rack rate quietly went up first.
2. **Read what's excluded before you celebrate.** "Free" Wi-Fi is sometimes basic-speed only; "included" drinks packages can exclude premium spirits; a "free" third guest still pays port fees and taxes.
3. **Don't panic-book in week one.** With the window now stretching four to six months, there's no need to jump on the first email. Compare two or three lines before you commit.
4. **Book through an Australian travel agent or the line's local site** so you're quoted in AUD from the start, rather than converting from a US dollar fare and getting a nasty surprise on your card statement.
5. **Ask specifically about onboard credit versus fare discount.** Onboard credit only has value if you'll actually spend it on the ship — a straight fare cut is worth more to a budget-conscious traveller who mostly enjoys the destinations, not the extras.

## The Bottom Line

If you're even loosely planning a cruise for later in the year — a Mediterranean run, an Alaska season, a lap of the Pacific — don't book on a whim in September. Wait for wave season, let the lines compete for your booking, and use the extended window to actually compare rather than panic. On a longer voyage, the difference between booking in July and booking during wave season can run into the thousands, not the hundreds. That's not a rounding error. That's a second holiday.

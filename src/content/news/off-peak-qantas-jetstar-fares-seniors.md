Here's the number that should make every retired traveller sit up: Sydney–Melbourne fares that run **$180 one-way** in the first week of the school holidays can fall to **$79 or less** just a fortnight either side of them. Same route, same airline, same seat — the only thing that changed is the calendar. If you're no longer chained to term dates, that gap is sitting there waiting for you.

## Your Biggest Advantage Is a Blank Calendar

Airlines price seats the way supermarkets price bananas — supply and demand, updated constantly. Families are locked into the last two weeks of school holidays and public holiday long weekends, so that's when Qantas and Jetstar know they can charge full freight. Retirees can fly on a random Tuesday in the middle of term, and that's exactly when the algorithm gets nervous and starts discounting empty seats.

- **Tuesdays and Wednesdays** are consistently the cheapest days to fly domestically — demand drops off a cliff once the Monday and Friday business crowd has moved through.
- **The first two weeks back at school** (early February, late April, mid-July, early October) are quietly some of the best value fares of the year, because business travel hasn't picked back up and families have gone home.
- **Avoid flying the Thursday or Friday before a long weekend**, and the Sunday after one — these are the two most expensive windows on the entire domestic network, sometimes 3-4 times the off-peak price.

## Qantas or Jetstar: Which Actually Saves You More?

Short answer: it depends what you value, and Marcus is not here to pretend one is universally "better."

**Jetstar** wins on headline price for point-to-point leisure routes — think Sydney to the Gold Coast or Melbourne to Perth — especially when you book into their regular **Friday Frenzy** sales. But the low fare is a starter price: checked baggage, seat selection and any flexibility cost extra, so a "cheap" fare can creep up fast if you need to change it.

**Qantas** rarely wins on the base fare, but for retirees who've built up years of **Qantas Frequent Flyer** points, the *Classic Rewards* seat releases on off-peak weekday flights can mean a capital city return trip for a fraction of the points you'd expect — sometimes as little as 8,000 points each way on quiet routes when released 355 days out. If you're sitting on a stash of unused points from a card you closed years ago, this is where they finally earn their keep.

## Five Ways to Stack the Savings

1. **Set a price alert, then wait it out.** Both airlines' fares move around before settling — a route you check today at $220 might show $140 by Thursday. Google Flights and the airlines' own apps will email you when a route drops.
2. **Book Tuesday morning.** Sale fares released over the weekend tend to be picked over by Monday night; airlines also frequently refresh inventory early in the week, which is when the best-priced seats reappear.
3. **Fly into the "second" airport.** Avalon instead of Melbourne, or Gold Coast instead of Brisbane, can undercut the primary airport by $30–$60 on Jetstar — worth it if a friend or family member is doing the driving at the other end.
4. **Check your Seniors Card before you check out.** Qantas doesn't offer a blanket Seniors Card airfare discount the way some regional or interstate rail and bus operators do, but Pensioner Concession Card holders should always ask at booking — some regional Queensland and Western Australian routes still carry concession fares worth confirming directly with the airline or a travel agent before you assume there's nothing on offer.
5. **Bundle the return, but book each leg separately when the numbers say to.** Return fares are usually cheaper as a pair, but on longer routes it's sometimes $40–$70 cheaper to fly Qantas outbound and Jetstar back (or vice versa) — the extra five minutes of comparing is worth the coffee money it saves.

## The Bottom Line

You don't need a travel hacking degree to fly cheaply within Australia — you need a flexible calendar and about ten minutes of comparison shopping, and retirees have both in spades. Skip the school holiday scramble, fly midweek where you can, and let the family with the rigid dates pay the premium fare while you take the $79 seat next to the window.

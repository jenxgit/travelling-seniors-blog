There is a particular kind of cruise brochure that I read the way other people read a map of a country they'll never visit — not quite planning a trip, just enjoying the shape of the thing. Cunard's World Voyage has always been exactly that kind of document. One hundred and fifteen nights. Five continents. A ship leaving Southampton in the northern winter and not coming home until the southern autumn. It is, by any sensible measure, not a holiday most of us have the time, the budget or the inclination to take in full.

Which is why the detail buried a few pages into Cunard's newly announced 2028–2029 season is the one worth your attention. *Queen Elizabeth* will indeed sail the full 115-night circumnavigation, departing Southampton on 23 January 2029 and returning on 17 May 2029, taking in 104 destinations across 49 countries and 64 UNESCO World Heritage Sites along the way, with overnight stays in Sydney, Singapore, Hong Kong and Cape Town. But Cunard has, sensibly, built the voyage with joining and leaving points along the route — and two of those sectors are built around Australia. You can step aboard here, sail somewhere genuinely extraordinary, and step off again without ever having signed up for the whole five months.

## The Two Sectors Worth Knowing About

The sector most directly aimed at Australian travellers is **Fremantle to Sydney**: nine nights, departing Fremantle on 16 March 2029, calling at Kangaroo Island, Melbourne and Burnie before an overnight stay in Sydney Harbour from 25 March. Fares start from $3,399 per person, twin share, for a lead interior cabin, with up to US$180 onboard credit per stateroom.

For those who'd rather arrive from the north, **Hong Kong to Fremantle** (10 nights, departing 6 March, from $3,499) and the longer **Hong Kong to Sydney** (19 nights, same departure, from $6,599) and **Singapore to Sydney** (24 nights, departing 1 March, from $8,499) all end — or begin — on Australian soil.

And for the genuinely adventurous, there's **Sydney to Los Angeles**: 28 nights departing 25 March 2029, crossing the Tasman to Auckland and Wellington before threading through the Pacific Islands, from $9,999 per person. Keep going and it extends to a 43-night **Sydney to Miami** sector for those who'd rather see the whole Pacific and Panama Canal crossing through to the other side of the Americas.

All fares quoted are the lead Interior rate, per person, twin share, and will climb for an ocean view or balcony cabin — Cunard's booking page or a cruise specialist can talk you through exactly how much, which I'd recommend doing before you set your heart on a particular sector.

## All Aboard, in the Right Week

Here is the part that actually requires you to put the kettle down and pay attention: bookings open in stages, all in Australian Eastern Daylight Time.

- **Tuesday 3 November 2026, 9am AEDT** — Cunard World Club members get first access to the World Voyage sectors sailing to and from Australia.
- **Wednesday 4 November 2026, 1am AEDT** — World Club members get access to the rest of the new 2028–29 season.
- **Thursday 5 November 2026, 1am AEDT** — general sale opens to everyone.

If you book between 3 November and midnight on 30 December 2026, Cunard is offering a 10% Early Booking Saving on eligible Cunard Fares, with an Early Saver Fare available as an alternative where it's offered. Given how quickly the popular sectors on a World Voyage tend to sell — and Fremantle to Sydney is about as popular as this gets for Australians — I would not treat the World Club pre-access window as optional reading. If you aren't already a member, joining is free and takes a few minutes, and it's worth doing well before 3 November rather than on the morning itself.

### Who This Suits

Readers who've always rather fancied a proper ocean crossing instead of a port-a-day itinerary; anyone drawn to Cunard's more formal, traditional style of service — think a dress code on gala nights and a genuine library, rather than a waterslide — who wants a joining point close to home instead of flying to Southampton; and travellers happy to commit to nine, nineteen or twenty-eight nights at a stretch without hopping off partway through.

### Who Should Skip It

If what you actually want is a resort ship with a dozen dining venues and entertainment running until midnight, Cunard was never going to be your line, World Voyage or otherwise. The Fremantle–Sydney sector's long open-ocean stretches won't suit anyone who gets restless without a port most days, and the 28-night Los Angeles sector is a genuine commitment of both time and budget that deserves real thought before you reserve.

## A Word on the Ship

*Queen Elizabeth* carries a little over 2,000 guests, which by modern cruising standards makes her a genuinely modest-sized ship — closer in spirit to the ocean liners of old than to the floating cities now common elsewhere in the industry. That matters for mobility: a smaller ship generally means shorter corridors between your cabin and the lifts, and less crowding at the muster stations and dining rooms than you'll find on a vessel carrying three or four times as many passengers. I'd still encourage anyone travelling with a mobility aid to call Cunard directly and confirm accessible cabin availability on your specific sector before you commit — these cabins are limited in number and, on a voyage this anticipated, will not stay available for long once booking opens.

### Before You Book

- Decide which sector actually fits your calendar and your appetite for time at sea before booking week arrives — nine nights and twenty-eight nights are very different holidays.
- Join the Cunard World Club now if you haven't already, so you're not locked out of the 3 November pre-access window.
- Ask your travel agent or Cunard directly about accessible cabins early if you need one; don't assume they'll still be there in week two.
- Confirm your fare includes the onboard credit and whether flights home from Los Angeles (if that's your sector) are bundled or need booking separately.
- Treat the quoted fares as a starting point, not a final figure — ocean view and balcony cabins, and any single-occupancy surcharge, will add to the lead interior price above.

For a trip most of us will only ever take once, there's something rather lovely about being able to dip into five months' worth of someone else's extraordinary voyage for a fortnight or so of your own. That, to my mind, is the whole point of a joining sector — all the romance of the crossing, none of the need to have cleared your diary until May.

Here's a number worth sitting with: **81% of Australian seniors say they're willing to cut back on other spending if that's what it takes to afford their next trip.** Not "would consider it" — willing, right now, as a matter of course. If you've ever quietly skipped a few coffees or put off replacing the lounge suite because a cruise deposit was due, you're not the exception. You're the overwhelming majority.

That figure comes from the **Grey Gap Year Report 2025**, commissioned by Australian Seniors and fielded by research firm MYMAVINS between 23 and 29 April 2025, surveying 1,206 Australians aged 50 and over. It's the most recent large-scale look at how older Australians are actually funding travel, and it's still the best data set going as I check this on 6 November 2026. A disclosure worth making up front: Australian Seniors sells travel insurance, so treat the insurance-related findings with a grain of salt — the spending and budget numbers aren't affected by that, and they're the ones I'm focused on here.

## What Seniors Are Actually Cutting

The survey asked directly: if you needed to adjust your budget to afford travel, what would you cut first? Multiple answers were allowed, so these don't add to 100%, but the pattern is clear.

- **Impulse or non-essential shopping** — 54%
- **Dining out or takeaway** — 53%
- **Clothing or fashion purchases** — 41%
- **Entertainment** (movies, concerts, events) — 40%
- **Home décor or non-essential household items** — 38%
- **Subscriptions** (streaming, magazines) — 33%
- **Technology or gadgets** — 32%
- **Coffee or daily treats** — 32%
- **Beauty or wellness services** (hair, spa, gym) — 30%
- **Home improvements or renovations** — 30%

Groceries don't appear anywhere on that list, and neither do gifts or downsizing — the survey didn't ask about them, so don't believe anyone who tells you "seniors are cutting back on food to travel." What they're actually doing is trimming the discretionary layer: the stuff that's nice to have, not the stuff that keeps the lights on.

## Which of These Cuts Are Actually Worth Making

This is where I'll give you my own two cents, because a list of percentages doesn't tell you what's sensible.

**Cut without a second thought:** impulse shopping and unused subscriptions. If you're not watching three streaming services a week or you can't remember the last thing you bought on a whim that you actually needed, that money is sitting there waiting to become a plane ticket. This is the easiest $20–$50 a month most households will ever find.

**Cut with moderation, not elimination:** dining out, coffee and daily treats, entertainment. These add real joy to ordinary life, and I'd rather you trimmed from three dinners out a week to one than cut them entirely and resent the trip for it. A $6 coffee five days a week is over $1,500 a year — cutting to three days a week still saves roughly $600, and nobody's life gets measurably worse for it.

**Think twice:** home improvements and renovations. Thirty per cent of seniors are willing to defer these to afford travel, and sometimes that's perfectly fine — a kitchen splashback can wait. But a leaking roof, a failing hot water system or dodgy wiring won't get cheaper for being ignored, and a $400 travel saving today can turn into a $4,000 repair bill next year. Separate "nice to have" renovations from "will cost more the longer I wait" maintenance before you decide what to defer.

**Your call entirely:** clothing, beauty and wellness services. Nobody else gets to tell you these don't matter to your quality of life. If a monthly hairdresser visit keeps you feeling like yourself, that's not an indulgence to be ashamed of — it's a budget line, same as any other, and you're allowed to weigh it against the holiday and choose either one.

## Where the Travel Money Actually Comes From

Cutting back is only half the picture — the survey also asked how seniors fund the trip itself, and savings dominate by a wide margin.

- **Savings: 68%** — still the main source, by far.
- **Spending the kids' inheritance: 10%**
- **Drawing down on superannuation: 8%**
- **Credit cards or personal loans: 7%**

On the inheritance question specifically, 68% say they're not worried about spending it and 69% feel no guilt about it at all — which tracks with 43% of seniors agreeing that creating travel memories matters more than leaving money behind. As for using retirement savings directly, 64% say they're comfortable doing so (19% very comfortable, 46% somewhat), while 19% have either delayed retirement or are weighing working longer specifically to afford more travel.

## How Much They're Actually Budgeting

Sixty-nine per cent of seniors set a formal travel budget each year. Here's how it breaks down:

- **Under $5,000 a year: 38%**
- **$5,000–$9,999 a year: 19%**
- **$10,000 or more a year: 12%**
- **No set budget at all: 31%**

Looking ahead, 44% expect that budget to stay about the same over the next five years, 18% expect it to grow, and 11% expect it to shrink. If you're in the "no budget" 31%, for what it's worth: setting even a rough annual figure is one of the simplest ways to turn "I'd love to travel more" into an actual plan, and it's exactly how the 69% who do set one are managing to keep travelling despite everything cost of living has thrown at them this year.

## The Cost-of-Living Reality Check

It isn't all good news. Seventy-one per cent of seniors say their travel plans have been affected by the rising cost of living over the past year, and 24% haven't made any travel plans at all because of it. Among those feeling the pinch, the most common responses were cutting the number of trips taken (20%), choosing more budget-friendly destinations (18%), travelling closer to home (18%), shortening trips (16%) and simply delaying plans (16%).

Ninety per cent of seniors agree holidays are becoming more expensive and harder to afford — which makes the 81% cutback figure make a lot more sense. People aren't trimming their budgets because travel got cheap. They're trimming because everything else got dearer and travel is the one thing they've decided not to sacrifice.

University of Queensland tourism researcher Gabby Walters, who worked on the report, says seniors are "choosing travel over other non-discretionary spending" as they plan their holidays. Her own summary of the mindset: "When it comes to travel, seniors are as old as they feel." Ninety-two per cent of those surveyed say they feel they've earned the right to spend their money freely and enjoy themselves on holiday — and on the numbers above, most of them are backing that belief with a genuine plan, not just a wish.

## Rosie's Bottom Line

If you're trying to find room in the budget for next year's trip, the survey suggests you're in good company, and it also suggests a sensible order of operations: cancel what you don't use, trim what you won't miss, protect anything that gets more expensive the longer it's deferred, and leave yourself the things that make ordinary life worth living in between trips. Then set an actual number for the year, even a modest one — the research shows the people who do that are the ones who keep travelling, cost of living or not.

This is general information based on published survey data, not financial advice tailored to your situation — your own budget, health and family circumstances are the final word on what's right for you.

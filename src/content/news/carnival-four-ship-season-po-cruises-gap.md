For the best part of two years, I have been fielding some version of the same letter: whatever happened to P&O? I understand the attachment entirely. For a great many of you, a P&O cruise out of Sydney or Brisbane was the first cruise you ever took, quite possibly booked by your own parents decades before I started writing this column. The brand that carried Australians to sea for ninety-odd years formally ceased trading in March 2025, its remaining ships folded into Carnival Cruise Line, which had owned P&O Cruises Australia outright since 2003. What has taken rather longer to settle is what actually replaces it. With Carnival's 2026-27 Australian season now locked in across four ships, I think we finally have a proper answer, and it is a bigger one than most of you are expecting.

## From Three Ships to Four

The two vessels that used to sail as Pacific Adventure and Pacific Encounter did not go anywhere; they simply changed their nameplates and livery, re-emerging as Carnival Adventure and Carnival Encounter, with their restaurants, public rooms and general layout carried over largely unchanged. The third member of the old P&O fleet, Pacific Explorer, was not so fortunate. She left the group entirely, sold on to Resorts World Cruises rather than staying in the Carnival family.

In their place, Carnival has sent reinforcements rather than a one-for-one swap. Carnival Splendor and Carnival Luminosa have joined the two renamed ships, taking the locally based fleet from three vessels to four — the largest Carnival presence this country has seen. Carnival Splendor and Carnival Adventure will both sail year-round out of Sydney; Carnival Encounter does the same from Brisbane. Carnival Luminosa rounds things out with a seasonal Brisbane posting through the Australian summer, after which she heads off to spend the rest of her year in Alaska.

### Before You Book: The Basics

- Carnival Splendor and Carnival Adventure sail year-round from Sydney.
- Carnival Encounter sails year-round from Brisbane.
- Carnival Luminosa sails from Brisbane seasonally, over the Australian summer only.
- Gratuities are not charged on Australian sailings on any of the four ships, unlike Carnival's US-based fleet — worth knowing if you are comparing a brochure fare against an old P&O one.

## Where the Four Ships Actually Go

This is where I'd encourage you to read past the "four ships" headline and look at the itineraries themselves, because they are not simply more of the same route repeated.

Carnival Splendor's Sydney program runs from two to twelve days. The shorter four-day sailings call at Moreton Island; five and six-day cruises head south to Tasmania, taking in Hobart and Port Arthur. For a longer trip, she also offers ten-day New Zealand itineraries with multiple departures, visiting Wellington, Lyttelton (for Christchurch) and Dunedin, with cruising through Fiordland National Park among the highlights — a gentler way to see Milford Sound than the famous day-tour coach convoys.

Carnival Adventure, also from Sydney, runs a similar two-to-twelve-day spread taking in Moreton Island and the Great Barrier Reef, with a notable addition: a twelve-day sailing to Fiji departing in April 2027, calling at Lautoka and Dravuni Island. Carnival Adventure and Carnival Splendor will both also run dedicated Melbourne Cup cruises, timed for Australia's most-watched race — a fixture many of my readers already build an annual trip around.

Carnival Luminosa, sailing from Brisbane on three-to-fourteen-day itineraries, includes a six-day Great Barrier Reef run with calls at Airlie Beach and Cairns. Carnival Encounter, also out of Brisbane, covers a similar spread of South Pacific and Reef destinations.

## Cabins, and a Word on Mobility

Carnival Adventure and Carnival Encounter keep their existing cabin categories and public spaces from their P&O days, so returning cruisers will find the layout familiar even with new branding on the door. Carnival Splendor and Carnival Luminosa are the two ships genuinely new to Australian waters, and both carry a reasonable number of accessible staterooms: Splendor has 26, split across fully accessible cabins for guests who use a wheelchair or scooter regularly, and ambulatory accessible cabins for those who use a cane or walker but don't need the full accessible layout; Luminosa carries 22 wheelchair-accessible cabins of her own.

One detail worth flagging before you book rather than after: wheelchair assistance from Carnival's crew is available during embarkation and disembarkation at the home port, but not for getting around once you're aboard or at a port of call. If that matters to your trip, it's worth planning for independently rather than assuming the ship will manage it for you.

### Who This Suits

Loyal P&O cruisers who want the same casual, family-friendly atmosphere and the same Sydney or Brisbane departure point, now with considerably more itinerary choice than the old three-ship fleet ever offered. Anyone drawn to the new longer options — the ten-day New Zealand run or the Fiji sailing in particular — who previously found P&O's own route list a bit thin for a proper holiday.

### Who Should Skip It

Anyone who specifically misses P&O's old onboard culture and expected Carnival to simply continue it under a new name. The ships, menus and activities lean more toward Carnival's broader, brasher house style than P&O's quieter Australian identity, and that shift is the one most of my correspondents actually write in about, more than the ships themselves.

## Loyalty: Starting From Zero, With a Twist

Here is the part that catches out even experienced cruisers. P&O Cruises Australia never ran its own proper loyalty scheme — points earned on board were historically tracked through Princess Cruises' Captain's Circle behind the scenes. Carnival has confirmed plainly that those points do not transfer across: any cruising you did under the P&O banner starts again from zero in Carnival's own program.

That program, in any case, has just changed its name and its rules. From 1 September 2026, Carnival's long-running VIFP Club became Carnival Rewards, moving from a system based purely on days sailed to one that also recognises what you spend on fares, onboard extras and pre-cruise bookings. If you were hoping your decades of P&O cruising would count for something on day one of your first Carnival sailing, I'm sorry to say it won't — but the new program at least means a big-spending first cruise now earns you more standing than the old days-counted system alone would have.

### Before You Book: Loyalty and Timing

- Past P&O sailings earn nothing under Carnival Rewards; everyone starts at the base tier regardless of cruising history.
- If you held meaningful status in Princess's Captain's Circle through past P&O cruises, that status remains valid there — it simply doesn't carry across to Carnival.
- Carnival Splendor and Carnival Luminosa are the ships to book if you want something genuinely new to this market rather than a renamed familiar ship.
- Confirm cabin accessibility details directly with Carnival's Guest Access team before booking if mobility is a consideration — the fleet-wide figures above are a starting point, not a guarantee for a specific sailing.

I'll admit I came to this story expecting to write something a little mournful, and there is genuine loss in it — a 90-year-old Australian brand doesn't disappear without leaving a gap. But four ships sailing more routes than the old fleet ever managed is not a story of decline, whatever it might feel like to a devoted P&O regular. The ships you knew are still there, mostly under new paint. The question worth asking before your next booking isn't whether P&O is coming back — it isn't — but which of the four current ships and their rather expanded route maps actually suits the holiday you're after.

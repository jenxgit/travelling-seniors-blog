I get a particular kind of letter every year, usually from a reader who has just discovered — somewhere around week seven of a lovely long trip to see the grandchildren in Manchester — that their payment has quietly changed shape while they weren't looking. They are rarely impressed. They are even less impressed when I explain that this isn't a mistake, an oversight, or a Centrelink conspiracy. It's simply what the portability rules say, and those rules have just been rewritten.

If you receive the Age Pension and you're planning time overseas — a six-week trip, a six-month trip, or a permanent sea change to somewhere warmer — there are now two separate sets of rules worth understanding before you book anything. One got noticeably more generous on 20 September 2026. The other got noticeably less so. Which one applies to you depends entirely on a single question: are you going, or are you *leaving*?

## The Big Thing That Hasn't Changed

Let's deal with the headline fact first, because it's the one most likely to be garbled by the time it reaches you secondhand: your basic Age Pension rate is not at risk just because you get on a plane. The Age Pension is one of the few Australian income support payments with indefinite portability, meaning you can, in principle, be paid it while overseas for as long as you like. That has not changed, and nothing below alters it.

What *can* change, for some pensioners, is the rate — and that's governed by a different, older rule than the one making headlines this year: your Australian Working Life Residence, or AWLR.

- If you lived in Australia for **35 years or more** between age 16 and Age Pension age, your pension rate generally stays at the full standard rate overseas, indefinitely, however long you're away.
- If you have **less than 35 years** of working-life residence, your rate is only guaranteed in full for the first 26 weeks overseas. After that, it's recalculated proportionally — based on your actual years of residence — using Centrelink's portability rate calculator.

This isn't new, and it isn't the thing that changed in September. But it's the rule most people have genuinely never heard of, and it's worth knowing your own AWLR figure (ask Centrelink, don't guess) before you commit to anything longer than a six-month stay.

## What Actually Changed on 20 September 2026

The real news this year concerns the **Pension Supplement** — the smaller top-up payment added to the base pension to help with everyday costs like utilities, phone and internet, and medicines. It is not the pension itself, but for many retirees it's a meaningful slice of the total payment, and the overseas rules around it have just moved in two different directions at once.

| Situation | Before 20 September 2026 | From 20 September 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| **Temporary trip overseas** (you intend to return) | Full Pension Supplement paid for the first **6 weeks**, then drops to the basic amount | Full Pension Supplement now paid for the first **12 weeks**, then drops to the basic amount |
| **Moving overseas permanently** | Pension Supplement drops to the basic amount from the day you leave | Pension Supplement **stops entirely** from the day you leave |
| **Base Age Pension rate** | Unaffected by either of the above (subject to the AWLR rule) | No change |

Read that table twice, because the two rows pull in opposite directions. If you're planning an extended holiday — say, three months doing the Trans-Siberian properly, or a long winter escaping to the Mediterranean — the change is good news. You now get double the time at the full supplement rate before it tapers off, which the Department of Social Services estimates benefits somewhere in the vicinity of 92,000 pensioners who travel without relocating.

If, on the other hand, you're one of the roughly 88,000 pensioners who have already made Spain, Bali or the Sunshine Coast of Portugal their permanent home — or you're planning to — the supplement doesn't taper anymore. It simply stops the day you go, rather than reducing to the basic amount as it used to. That's a real cut to your total payment, and it applies regardless of how long you've been getting it.

## So Which Rule Applies to You?

The whole system hinges on a distinction Centrelink takes seriously even when your own plans are genuinely fuzzy: are you *travelling* or are you *moving*? This isn't always as obvious as it sounds, particularly for retirees doing what I'd call the "extended trial run" — six months in Thailand to see how it feels before deciding whether to make it permanent.

My plain advice, developed from years of reading other people's unhappy letters: don't let Centrelink find out what your trip "really was" after the fact. Tell them in advance.

1. **Decide, honestly, what kind of trip this is** — a holiday with a return date, or a move with no fixed return — before you notify Centrelink, not after.
2. **Update your travel details through myGov or the Centrelink app** before departure, including your intended length of absence. This is also where any future change in plans — deciding partway through to stay longer, or to stay permanently — needs to be reported, not left for Centrelink to discover when a letter bounces.
3. **Check your own AWLR figure** if you're planning to be away longer than 26 weeks and haven't lived in Australia for 35-plus years between 16 and Age Pension age. It determines your rate, and it's not something to estimate from memory.
4. **Call the Centrelink international services line before, not during, a long trip** if your plans are genuinely undecided. A ten-minute phone call is considerably less stressful than an unexpected rate change discovered from an app notification in a foreign time zone.
5. **Know that coming home resets some clocks and not others.** If you return to Australia for more than 26 weeks before heading off again, the standard residency rules apply fresh, as they would to a new absence. There's also no waiting period to re-claim the Age Pension if you've been living overseas and come home for good — a detail that surprises people who assume re-entry involves some kind of bureaucratic penalty box. It doesn't.

## A Word on Why This Matters More Than It Used to

Australians over 65 are travelling overseas at a higher rate than at almost any point I can recall covering this topic, and a growing number are doing it for months at a time rather than the traditional fortnight. That's entirely sensible — long, slow trips suit retirement far better than a rushed sprint through six countries in nine days — but it does mean more pensioners than ever are brushing up against rules that, until now, applied mostly to a small number of permanent expats.

If your trip is temporary, the September changes work in your favour, and there's genuinely nothing to do except enjoy the extra six weeks of breathing room. If you're weighing up a permanent move — to be closer to family overseas, for the climate, or simply for a cheaper retirement — the calculation has changed, and it's worth sitting down with the actual numbers from Services Australia before you sign a lease or sell a house, not after.

## The Bottom Line

The Age Pension itself isn't going anywhere just because you are. What changes — sometimes for the better, sometimes considerably for the worse — is the supplement riding alongside it, and which rule applies depends on whether Centrelink has you filed as a traveller or a leaver. Work out which one you genuinely are, tell them in advance, and check your own AWLR if a long trip is on the cards. Do that, and the only letter you'll need to write me is one about the weather in Manchester.

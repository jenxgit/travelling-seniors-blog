Here's the number worth paying attention to: the Aussie dollar is buying around **62 euro cents** as this goes to print, up from a six-month low of under **60 cents** back in March. That's roughly a 4% swing in your favour in half a year, and if you're weighing up a European river cruise or escorted tour for 2026-27, that difference lands directly in your pocket — or it would, if you know how to actually capture it before it moves again.

## The Numbers Behind the Headline

Currency movements sound abstract right up until you're staring at a $9,000 cruise invoice, so let's make this concrete.

- **AUD/EUR** has climbed from a low of **0.5961** in late March 2026 to a recent high above **0.62**, averaging around **0.611** over the past six months. That's real, not noise.
- **AUD/USD** has moved even further — from **0.6676** in January to a 2026 high near **0.726** in May, and sitting close to **0.72** as I write this. It's up more than 5% for the year.
- Why does the US dollar matter for a *European* holiday? Because most of the big international cruise lines and tour operators sailing the Rhine, the Danube and the Mediterranean — Viking, Scenic, Riviera, the major ocean lines — price and hedge their product in USD even when your invoice lands in your inbox in Australian dollars. A stronger AUD against **both** currencies is a genuine tailwind on two fronts, not one.

One detailed breakdown by *Cruise Passenger* found a family's total cruise bill came in **$883 cheaper** than the same booking would have cost just months earlier, purely from the exchange rate move — no sale, no discount code, nothing but the calendar and the currency doing the work.

## Why the Saving Doesn't Show Up Automatically

Here's the bit operators don't put in the brochure: your fare doesn't quietly get cheaper every time the AUD ticks up. Most European tour and cruise operators set their AUD selling price against the exchange rate at a fixed point — usually when the brochure or season's pricing is released — and then hold that price for months regardless of what the currency does afterwards. That cuts both ways:

- If the AUD **weakens** after the price is set, you're protected — you paid the better rate.
- If the AUD **strengthens** after the price is set, as it has been doing since March, the operator generally keeps the margin rather than passing it back to you.

Which means the saving from a stronger dollar isn't sitting there waiting for you inside an existing quote — you generally only capture it in one of a few specific ways.

## Five Ways to Actually Capture the Advantage

1. **Book and deposit now, don't wait for it to get "even better."** New season pricing for 2026-27 Europe departures is being released against something close to current rates. Waiting six months on the hope the AUD climbs further means betting against a currency that can turn on a single Reserve Bank statement — and it has done exactly that twice already this year.
2. **Pay the balance early if your operator allows it.** Many river cruise lines let you pay out the full fare ahead of the due date. If you're confident in the trip, locking today's exchange rate in against the whole invoice — not just the deposit — is how you actually bank the saving rather than just admire it.
3. **Ask specifically whether your fare was priced against a recent rate.** Operators refresh their AUD pricing on different schedules — some monthly, some seasonally. A fare quoted last week is a better reflection of today's dollar than one that's been sitting in a brochure since February.
4. **Pre-purchase what you can in AUD before you fly.** Onboard credit, drink packages, shore excursions and gratuities are typically sold in AUD if you buy them through the Australian booking office ahead of time, rather than charged in USD or EUR once you're onboard or in port. Locking these in now means a favourable rate covers your whole trip, not just the airfare and cabin.
5. **Use a genuine no-fee travel card for the spending money you'll carry.** None of the exchange rate advantage matters if you hand it straight back through a 3% card surcharge or a terrible airport kiosk rate. A card with no international transaction fees, loaded or drawn down at the interbank-adjacent rate, keeps the saving intact for every coffee, museum ticket and market souvenir.

## Marcus's Honest Caveat

I'm not a currency forecaster and neither is anyone else who tells you with total confidence where the Aussie dollar sits in twelve months — I've been doing this column long enough to have watched plenty of "sure things" go the other way. What I can tell you is where the dollar has actually been: meaningfully stronger against both the euro and the US dollar than it was at the start of the year, driven largely by a weaker US dollar and firm commodity prices propping up ours.

That's not a guarantee it stays this way. It's a genuinely favourable window, not a one-way bet, and the sensible move is the same one that works in any market: lock in the good rate on the things you're already planning to buy, rather than trying to time the absolute peak. Nobody, including Marcus, picks the peak.

## The Bottom Line

A 4-5% currency tailwind on a $8,000-$15,000 European cruise or tour package is $300-$700 of real money — before you've compared a single itinerary or chased a single sale. Combine it with paying early to lock the rate, pre-purchasing extras in AUD, and carrying a genuinely fee-free card, and the saving compounds rather than evaporates the moment you land in Europe.

**Before you book, always:**

- Check when your quoted fare was actually priced against the exchange rate, not just when you received it.
- Ask your operator whether early full payment is allowed and whether it locks in the current AUD rate.
- Pre-purchase onboard credit, excursions and gratuities in AUD wherever the option exists.
- Compare travel money cards for genuine no international transaction fees before you load one up.
- Remember this is a favourable window, not a forecast — don't stretch the budget on the promise the dollar keeps climbing.

The Aussie dollar won't sit this strong forever, and nobody rings a bell when it turns. If a European trip has been on the list, the numbers right now are doing you a favour — the trick is actually banking it before they change their mind.

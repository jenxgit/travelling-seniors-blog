Thailand has long been one of the most reliably simple countries for Australians to visit — turn up, get a stamp, stay two months. As of 15 September 2026, that has changed on three fronts at once, and none of the changes were particularly well publicised before they landed. Given how many of us treat Thailand as a regular fixture rather than a once-off trip, it is worth setting the record straight.

## What's Actually Changed

| Rule | Before | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Visa-free stay for Australians | Up to 60 days | Up to **30 days** |
| Arrival paperwork | Paper arrival card, or none | **Mandatory digital arrival card (TDAC)** |
| Tourist tax | None | **Proposed** ~450 baht (roughly AUD $20), expected early-to-mid 2027 |

Let me take each in turn, because the practical implications differ considerably.

### 1. The Visa-Free Stay Has Been Halved

Australians travelling on an ordinary tourist trip no longer receive the 60-day visa exemption on arrival — it is now capped at **30 days**. For the overwhelming majority of readers booking a two- or three-week holiday, this changes precisely nothing. It matters considerably more if you are among the growing number of retirees who have taken to spending an entire Thai winter in Hua Hin or Chiang Mai. If your planned stay runs longer than 30 days, you will now need to arrange a proper visa in advance through the Thai consulate rather than relying on the exemption — do this before you fly, not after you've already overstayed, since overstay penalties in Thailand are enforced without much sentiment.

### 2. The Digital Arrival Card Is No Longer Optional

The Thailand Digital Arrival Card, or TDAC, has technically existed since May 2025, but it is now firmly and universally required. You must complete it online within **three days before arrival** — not on landing, not at the airport kiosk in a rush, but in advance, from home, with your details at hand. It asks for:

1. Passport details
2. Flight numbers, both arriving and departing
3. Your accommodation address in Thailand
4. A basic health declaration
5. Standard personal and travel-date information

Do this the week before you fly, print or screenshot the confirmation, and keep it with your passport. Immigration officers have shown limited patience for travellers attempting to fill it out on their phone in the queue.

### 3. A Tourist Tax Is Coming, Eventually

A tax of roughly 450 baht — call it AUD $20 — has been proposed and is expected to take effect sometime in early-to-mid 2027. As of this writing, exactly how it will be collected remains unresolved: candidates include folding it into the airline ticket price, a dedicated website or app, or airport kiosks on arrival. It is not yet in force, so there is nothing to action today beyond noting that your next Thailand trip, if booked for 2027 or later, may well carry this modest additional cost. I will update readers once the mechanism is confirmed, rather than have anyone chase a tax that doesn't yet exist.

## What This Means for Your Next Trip

If you're planning a standard two-to-four week holiday, the practical change is singular and manageable: complete the TDAC online in the days before you fly. If you're planning an extended stay beyond 30 days, start the visa conversation with a Thai consulate or a reputable visa agent well before booking flights, since visa processing has its own timeline that a departure date won't wait for.

One industry observer described the 30-day change as "unlikely to make much difference" for the average Australian holidaymaker, and I'm inclined to agree — provided you know about it in the first place, which, until now, is precisely the problem this article exists to solve.
